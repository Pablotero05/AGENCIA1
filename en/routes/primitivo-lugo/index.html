Route description

Primitive Way from Lugo

The Primitive Way is the original Jacobean route, first walked by King Alfonso II the Chaste in the 9th century. Its section from Lugo spans the required 100+ km to receive the Compostela.

The pilgrimage begins passing through the UNESCO World Heritage Roman Wall of Lugo, continuing along quiet country trails, authentic hamlets, and ancient forests.

In Melide, the Primitive Way joins the bustling French Way, offering vibrant pilgrim culture and great regional food before reaching the Cathedral in Santiago de Compostela.

Highlights

  • Walking atop the Roman Wall of Lugo (UNESCO World Heritage Site)
  • Traditional hamlets and rustic paths of San Román da Retorta
  • Merging with the French Way in Melide and tasting traditional Galician octopus
  • Shady eucalyptus and oak forests around Arzúa and O Pedrouzo
  • First glimpse of the Cathedral towers from Monte do Gozo
  • Unforgettable finish in Plaza del Obradoiro in Santiago de Compostela

Day by day planning

Recommended Stages

1

Lugo → San Román da Retorta

Departure from the Roman wall of Lugo. Peaceful stage featuring rural tracks, woods, and tiny hamlets. Perfect to begin without excessive strain.

19–20 km 4h30–5 h Difficulty Low
2

San Román da Retorta → Melide

Long and somewhat demanding stage. Deep rural environment and a feeling of solitude. Arrival in Melide where the Primitive merges with the French Way.

27–28 km 6–7 h Difficulty High
3

Melide → Arzúa

Short and comfortable stage, ideal for recovery. Plenty of forest, solid terrain, and more pilgrims upon joining the French Way. Finishes in Arzúa.

14–15 km 3–4 h Difficulty Low
4

Arzúa → O Pedrouzo

Pleasant path through eucalyptus groves and well-kept trails. Gentle uphills. Arrival in O Pedrouzo, classic stop before the final day.

19 km 4h30–5 h Difficulty Medium
5

O Pedrouzo → Santiago de Compostela

Final stage with a mix of woodlands and urban approaches. Memorable arrival in Santiago de Compostela.

20 km 5 h Difficulty Medium
